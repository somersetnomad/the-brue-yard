Reading the valley with your feet

By the curatorial team

The Brue drops some forty metres between the Bruton weir and the meadow’s eastern hedge. You feel that fall before you see it. Echoes Across the Valley begins from that sensation: sculpture as a way of measuring ground, and the ground as a record of every hand that has worked it.

In the Threshing Barn, cast forms hold the negative space of rooms and stairwells, set against the barn’s own worn threshing floor. Out on the North Meadow, walked lines and gathered stone answer the river’s contours. Four new commissions sit on the banks themselves, each placed where the flood line of 1917 once reached.

In the exhibition

Artists

Two artists who have spent careers measuring land with their hands, and four new works made for this river.

  • The Threshing Barn

    Rachel Whiteread

    Casts the spaces inside and beneath things: rooms, stairs, the gap under a chair. Works in plaster, resin and bronze stand on the barn’s original threshing floor.

  • The North Meadow

    Richard Long

    Makes work by walking, from lines worn into grass to stones gathered and set out in lines and circles. His work here runs from the barn door down to the water.

  • The river banks

    Four new commissions

    Chosen through an open call to artists in the South West, and placed along the river path where the 1917 flood line reached.
